## Which coverage definition was used

Two are reported and they are **not** the same number:

- **Coverage, fixed N = 16 — the gated one.** A second pass over the same 200
  runs with `SequentialConfig(batch=16, max_n=16)`: a single look at the full
  budget, no early stopping. Coverage = the fraction of tested (run, step) pairs
  whose reported 95% interval contains the step's true effect
  `treated_prob(k) − control_prob`. **0.9671.**
- **Coverage, sequential — report only, not gated.** The same fraction for the
  interval as the sequential procedure reports it at whatever N it stopped at.
  **0.9796.**

Both passes use the same sampler seeds, so they share an identical control arm
per run and are paired. Nothing was adjusted for multiplicity; the pre-
registration says to measure the cost of peeking, not to correct for it.

Note the direction of the sequential number: it is *higher* than nominal, not
lower. Stopping early is triggered by an interval that has moved decisively away
from δ, and those stopped intervals are wide (small N), so they still tend to
cover the truth. The damage from peeking shows up in the **decision** (criterion
a), not in the interval's coverage — which is exactly why criterion (a) and
criterion (b) can disagree, and why coverage alone would have been a misleading
gate.

## Cost

| | sequential | fixed N = 16 |
|---|---|---|
| mean re-runs per run | 297.9 | 317.7 |
| saving from early stopping | **6.2%** | — |

The saving is small, and the reason is structural rather than incidental: with
δ = 0.10 and N ≤ 16, a truly-null step's interval is almost never narrow enough
to be *cleared* early. 3140 of 3771 step-tests ran the full four batches. Early
stopping pays only where a step is decisively blame-worthy, which is roughly one
step per run. Cost accounting for a shared control at ~19 tested steps is
`19 × 16 + 16 = 320` re-runs, matching the fixed column.

## Why this is FAILED and not a bug (0 of 3 fix attempts used)

The two failure modes are separable, and the fixed-N diagnosis separates them.

**1. Power, ~8 points.** At a fixed N = 16 per arm the planted step is found in
only **184/200 = 0.920** of runs, with **all 16 misses being `none`** — the
planted step's interval never cleared δ, and *no* false blame occurred
(0/1597 null steps flagged). A Newcombe interval on 16 vs 16 draws is simply
wide: a true effect of 0.53 (treated 10/16 against control 2/16) yields a lower
bound of +0.053, below δ = 0.10. The pre-registered N = 16 caps achievable
accuracy at about 92% under this generative model, before any sequential
testing is considered. **No estimator change can fix this; only a larger N
can.**

**2. Peeking, ~3 more points.** Sequential stopping turns 0 false blames into 9
(184 → 178). Every single one of those 9 was blamed at the **first look, n = 4**,
and in every one of the 9 the fixed-N design blames the correct planted step.
The per-step false-positive rate on truly-null steps is 10/1597 = **0.63%**
sequentially versus **0.00%** at fixed N. With ~8 null steps ahead of the
planted step per run, 0.63% per step ≈ 4.9% of runs lost — which matches the
observed 9/200 = 4.5%. The mechanism is exactly the one flagged in
`docs/decisions/0005-shared-control.md`: a 3/4 or 4/4 treated arm at the first
look, measured against a shared control that is often 0/16, clears δ by chance,
and the earliest-step rule then locks that false positive in. 7 of the 9 had a
shared control of 0/16 or 2/16.

**Evidence that the estimator itself is correct**, i.e. that these are
properties of the pre-registered method and not defects:

- Both intervals reproduce published reference values to 5e-5: Wilson 81/263 →
  [0.2553, 0.3662]; Newcombe method 10, 56/70 vs 48/80 → [0.0524, 0.3339]. The
  Wilson implementation is also checked against an independent derivation from
  the roots of the score equation, at 1e-12.
- Fixed-N coverage of 0.9671 is the known, slightly conservative behaviour of
  Newcombe's method 10 at small n. A miscomputed interval would not land inside
  the [0.92, 0.98] window.
- No planted step was ever stopped early as "cleared" — every `none` miss ran
  the full N = 16, so the failure is not premature stopping on the true step.
- The generator's ground truth is self-consistent: a property test asserts that
  the earliest step whose *true* effect exceeds δ is exactly the planted step in
  every generated run, so the blame rule and the label cannot disagree by
  construction.

Accordingly no threshold, δ, N, batch size, generative parameter or seed was
touched after seeing the result, and none of the three permitted bug-fix
attempts was spent. The report-only diagnosis fields (`found at fixed N=16`,
`null steps flagged`) were added to the gate script *after* the first failing
run, purely to explain it; they changed no criterion and no gated number — the
gated numbers are byte-identical before and after.

## What would have to change (a decision for P5, not a fix here)

None of these may be applied retroactively to make P4 pass. Recorded for the
phase that has to live with the consequence:

1. **Raise N.** N = 16 caps accuracy near 0.92 here. The gate's 0.95 bar and the
   pre-registered N = 16 are, under this generative model, mutually
   inconsistent — the bar was set without a power calculation.
2. **Do not act on the first look.** Requiring a minimum N before a step may be
   declared blame-worthy, or an alpha-spending boundary, would recover the ~3
   points lost to peeking at n = 4 at no extra cost.
3. **A judge shortlist helps here.** P4 tests ~19 steps per run; Bisect proper
   tests m = 3. Fewer null steps means proportionally fewer chances at a false
   early blame — so P5's accuracy on the same estimator should be *better* than
   this number, not worse, provided the judge's recall@m holds.
