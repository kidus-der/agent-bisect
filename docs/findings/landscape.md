### What is genuinely new in Bisect vs. prior art

- No item found combines all of: (1) deterministic world-state snapshot + rewind, (2) an actual **designed intervention** replayed **N times as treated vs. control** (not just K forward rollouts of one condition), (3) **Newcombe CIs** on the treated/control effect difference specifically, (4) an **earliest-step-whose-CI-lower-bound-clears-δ** blame rule, (5) an LLM judge used only to **shortlist** suspects (cost control) rather than to attribute directly, (6) evaluation on **planted faults in τ²-bench**, and (7) shipping as **CLI + dashboard + GitHub Action PR check**.
- CAR is the closest on statistical mechanism (do-operator, repeated re-runs, per-step CIs) but stays in synthetic-SCM validation, ships no CI/dashboard product, and isn't evaluated on τ²-bench.
- agentoptics/rewind is the closest on product shape (CLI + fork/replay + GH Action) but is deterministic/LLM-opinion-based, not statistical.
- AgenTracer already owns the phrase "counterfactual replay + programmed fault injection," but uses it to manufacture *training data* for a judge model, not as a live debugging/CI signal with a formal blame rule.
- τ²-bench appears not yet paired with a published fault-injection + attribution study — worth confirming again before claiming it as a first, since this space moves in ~monthly arXiv drops.

### Risks of being scooped / overlaps to acknowledge

- CAR (2606.08275) is mechanistically close enough that a follow-up revision adding a real benchmark + tooling would erode much of Bisect's claimed novelty; cite it explicitly and lead with the δ-threshold blame rule + judge-shortlist + τ²-bench eval + shipped product as the differentiators.
- AgenTracer's fault-injection framing should be cited and contrasted (train-a-judge vs. live-debug-a-PR) rather than left unacknowledged.
- FAMAS (spectrum analysis) is conceptually adjacent (statistics over repeated executions) even though the formula family (SBFL suspiciousness) differs from Bisect's CI/δ approach.
- agentoptics/rewind already occupies the "CLI + GitHub Action for agent debugging" product niche; Bisect needs to message the statistical rigor clearly to avoid being read as "another rewind."
- This is a fast-moving space: at least 8 closely related arXiv papers dated between 2025-09 and 2026-07 surfaced in one search pass. Re-run this check again before the report/paper is finalized, and again before any public launch.

### 1. Reconciliation: Who&When Pro 73.9% vs. the 36.2% baseline number

**Verdict: both numbers are real, but they are not comparable, and 73.9% is not a like-for-like beat of 36.2% or of the original Who&When 14.2%.**

- Paper confirmed: **"Who&When Pro: Can LLMs Really Attribute Failures in AI Agents?"**, Jiale Liu, Huajun Xi, Shaokun Zhang, Yifan Zeng, Tianwei Yue, Chi Wang, Jian Kang, Qingyun Wu, Huazheng Wang; submitted 2026-07-10. [arXiv:2607.09996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09996) / [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09996v1).
- Dataset: 12,326 failed trajectories, 26 source benchmarks, 3 modalities (text/image/video), 9 task categories — built by taking a **successful** trajectory, injecting one error at a chosen step *t* via a frontier-model-generated "adaptive injection prompt," **warm-starting** replay of steps 1..t-1 (content-addressable cache for static tool calls), then letting the agent continue live from the injected action. 18 error modes across perception/reasoning/planning/action/verification/coordination. Human review confirms 94% step-label and 90% agent+error-family-label accuracy.
- **73.9% is the step-level accuracy of the best model (Qwen3.5-122B) on the text-only subset, under the "all-at-once" attribution protocol** (Table 4, per WebFetch of the HTML paper). The same model scores only **22.2% macro-F1 on error-mode classification** — confirms the brief's pairing of these two numbers is accurate. GPT-5.4 leads step identification specifically; GLM-5 leads joint accuracy on text; these are close but not identical numbers depending on which sub-metric is quoted — 73.9% is the one the brief cites and it does check out.
- **Why this is not comparable to the original Who&When's 14.2% or to Ma et al.'s 36.2%:** the *construction method itself is easier for a judge*. Because the injected trajectory is a **verbatim replay of a real success up to step t**, and only step t's action is adversarially rewritten, a judge doing "all-at-once" attribution over the full 12,326-trajectory corpus is essentially doing anomaly-detection over a mostly-normal trace with one synthetically-inserted outlier action — the injection method leaves detectable artifacts. Original Who&When's 184 traces are **organic failures from real deployed MAS runs** with no synthetic tell, across only 2 benchmarks — a much harder, noisier attribution target. The paper itself frames the gulf between 73.9% step accuracy and 22.2% error-mode F1 as its headline finding (a judge can often point at *the* rewritten step without understanding *why* it's wrong), not as evidence that failure attribution is "solved." Treat 73.9% and 14.2%/36.2% as measuring different constructs (synthetic-injection localization vs. organic-failure attribution), not as points on the same leaderboard.
- **Overlap with our planted-fault construction:** real and worth citing explicitly. Who&When Pro's recipe — record a success, pick a step, inject one adversarial error there, warm-start/replay the prefix, run the rest live, keep it if the injection actually breaks the trajectory — is structurally the same shape as our `bench/inject.py` five-step recipe (record success → stability check → pick k stratified early/middle/late → plant one of four faults → re-run N=4 → keep if faulted pass ≤ 0.25). Differences to state in the related-work table: (a) their injection is LLM-generated and adversarial/semantic across 18 error modes vs. our four mechanical fault types (wrong value / missing field / stale record / tool error text) on tool *results* specifically; (b) they evaluate attribution as a single **judge call over the resulting trace** (no re-run, no control arm, no CI) whereas Bisect's blame step is a **statistical estimate from repeated re-runs vs. a control**; (c) their scale (12,326) dwarfs our target (≥120) but is multi-modal/general-agent, not tool-call-and-database grounded like τ²-bench, so pass/fail is judged rather than reward-computed by an environment.

### 2. New since the P0 baseline

- **TrajDebug / TrajErrBench** — [arXiv:2608.06346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06346) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06346), [GitHub: THU-KEG/TrajDebug](https://github.com/THU-KEG/TrajDebug)), EMNLP 2026 Findings. **This closes the τ²-bench gap the baseline flagged as open.** TrajErrBench is 486 **manually annotated** (not fault-injected) failed trajectories drawn from **Tau2Bench and SWE-Bench Pro**. TrajDebug is a three-stage, purely **log-based/observational** pipeline (error-trigger detection with verbatim evidence → error-state classification → causal attribution that "selects the failure-responsible origin from terminal-relevant candidates") aiming at the same target as Bisect's blame rule — "the earliest error step... responsible for the final failure" — but via LLM trace-reading, with **no rewind, no re-execution, no intervention, no CI**. Reported to beat LLM-prompting baselines and existing diagnostic systems on TrajErrBench and other benchmarks (exact headline accuracy number UNVERIFIED — not surfaced in the fetched summaries; pull from the paper before citing a number). Must cite: it is the first attribution work we've found grounded in τ²-bench specifically, but it uses organic failures and observation only, so it does not compete with Bisect's counterfactual/interventional design — cite as "closest benchmark overlap, no method overlap."
- **Causal Attribution for Agentic Decisions: Estimators, Coupling, and a Traceability Specification** — [arXiv:2609.06445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06445) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06445)), single author (Ajay Pravin Mahale, Hochschule Trier), submitted 2026-09-06 — 11 days before this check. Theoretical/regulatory paper (motivated by EU AI Act traceability requirements for high-risk systems, effective Aug 2026): shows naive per-step estimators can make a causally inert step look identical to a decisive one, and proposes a **common-random-numbers (CRN) coupled estimator** that isolates a step's own contribution by sharing randomness across the treated/control rollouts, plus a traceability record spec. **No confidence intervals, no blame rule, and explicitly no empirical evaluation** — the author states the "discrepancy experiment" is pre-registered but wasn't run ("the live pipeline it required was not available in the study window"). Worth a methodological citation and a self-check: Bisect's treated/control arms currently use independent Wilson/Newcombe CIs on separately-sampled proportions rather than CRN-coupled (shared-seed) sampling; this paper argues CRN gives tighter isolation of a step's own effect. Not a competitive threat (no working system, no benchmark), but flag as related-work and consider whether seeding treated/control from the same RNG stream where the harness allows it would strengthen Bisect's own estimator story.
- **CAR, agentoptics/rewind, DoVer, CausalFlow, AgenTracer, Mirrors:** no material change found. CAR (arXiv:2606.08275) is unchanged — no new benchmark eval surfaced, still synthetic-SCM only. agentoptics/rewind is unchanged in kind: replay + LLM-judge "eval score" for fix verification, still no statistical CI mode found. DoVer's 2026 description (checkpoint/re-plan segmentation + log-based-hypothesis-then-verify) matches the baseline entry, no new numbers surfaced. CausalFlow (arXiv:2605.25338) confirmed published at the ICLR 2026 "Agents in the Wild" workshop, evaluated on 4 benchmarks (math reasoning, code gen, QA, medical browsing) — none of which is τ²-bench; no material change to its baseline entry. AgenTracer: only referenced by newer papers in this pass, no new release found. **Mirrors** (YC, [runmirrors.com](https://www.runmirrors.com/)) is now clearly characterized: it fabricates a runnable twin of an agent's tools/APIs from production traces and replays sessions against tool/prompt/model changes in CI, diffing tool calls — this is environment-mocking for regression testing, not step-level causal attribution; no step blame, no CIs. Matches/extends (not contradicts) the baseline's one-line entry.
- **AgentChaosBench** (surfaced incidentally via [arXiv:2608.14680](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14680), "When Agentic Executions Fail: Detecting and Localizing Runtime Faults from Telemetry") — a fault-injection benchmark injecting faults at tool-call/LLM-call/guardrail/agent-to-agent boundaries, built on AI-NativeBench workloads (not τ²-bench). Adjacent but off-target (different benchmark substrate, telemetry-localization framing rather than replay-based attribution); log for completeness, not added to the prior-art table this pass — UNVERIFIED whether it does re-execution or is purely detection-from-logs.

### Implications for the report's related-work table and citable numbers

- **Add TrajDebug/TrajErrBench to the prior-art table** (`docs/brief/summary.md` §8 style: Snapshots ✗ · Replay ✗ (log-based only) · Causal stats ◐ (rule-based origin selection, no CI) · Benchmark ✓ (τ²-bench + SWE-Bench Pro) · PR check ✗) with the note "first published τ²-bench attribution benchmark; organic failures, observational method, no intervention — cite as the closest *benchmark* overlap, not a *method* overlap."

Keep 36.2% (Ma et al.) as the step-level bar on organic-failure benchmarks (Who&When/TRAIL), since that is the comparable-construct number.
- **Cite CRN coupling (arXiv:2609.06445) as a methodological note**, not a competitor: flag in the report's limitations/future-work section that independent-sample Wilson/Newcombe CIs are one valid design choice but CRN-coupled sampling is a candidate refinement, with a pointer to this paper.
